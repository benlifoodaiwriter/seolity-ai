# Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content

Google's ranking systems prioritize helpful, reliable content created to benefit people rather than to manipulate rankings. This guidance covers self-assessing content quality, demonstrating E-E-A-T, avoiding search engine-first practices, and being transparent about who created content, how, and why.

## Self-assess content quality

- Do evaluate each page against quality questions: does it offer original information, reporting, research or analysis, and a substantial, complete description of the topic? This helps you judge whether it is helpful and reliable.
- Do add substantial value and originality when drawing on other sources. Avoid simply copying or rewriting them, because that adds little for readers.
- Do write a main heading or page title that is a descriptive, helpful summary of the content. Avoid exaggerated or shocking titles.
- Do compare the page with other results for the same search and make sure it provides substantial value beyond them.
- Avoid spelling and stylistic issues and sloppy, hastily produced content, because they undermine perceived quality and trust.
- Avoid mass-producing content or spreading it across a large network of sites or outsourcing it to many creators, so that individual pages don't receive less care.
- Do get an honest assessment from trusted people unaffiliated with your site, and audit pages and search types affected by any traffic drops against these questions.

## Focus on people-first content

- Do create content primarily for an existing or intended audience that would find it useful if they came directly to your site.
- Do demonstrate first-hand experience and depth of knowledge, such as having actually used a product or service or visited a place.
- Do keep your site focused on a primary purpose or topic area.
- Do make sure readers leave feeling they have learned enough to achieve their goal and had a satisfying experience.
- Do apply SEO to people-first content: practices that help search engines discover and understand content are helpful when the content itself is made for people.

## Avoid search engine-first practices

- Avoid producing lots of content on many unrelated topics in hope that some will rank, and avoid chasing trends your audience doesn't care about.
- Avoid using extensive automation to produce content on many topics, and avoid merely summarizing what others say without adding value.
- Avoid writing to a particular word count based on the belief that Google has a preferred length, because it doesn't.
- Avoid entering a niche topic without real expertise solely to get search traffic.
- Avoid promising answers to questions that have no answer, such as implying a release date for a product, movie or show when none is confirmed.
- Avoid changing page dates to appear fresh when the content hasn't substantially changed.
- Avoid adding or removing large amounts of content just to make the site seem "fresh", because that won't improve rankings.
- Avoid content that leaves readers needing to search again elsewhere for better information.

## Demonstrate E-E-A-T

- Do show trust signals such as clear sourcing, evidence of expertise, and background about the author and the site, for example links to an author page or an About page. Trust is the most important aspect of E-E-A-T.
- Do have content written or reviewed by an expert or enthusiast who demonstrably knows the topic, and avoid easily verified factual errors.
- Do apply extra rigor to YMYL topics (health, financial stability, safety, societal welfare): content must be highly accurate and consistent with established expert consensus, since these topics receive more weight on E-E-A-T.
- Do not treat E-E-A-T as a specific ranking factor; use it as a lens, and use the search quality rater guidelines to self-assess. Content doesn't need to show every aspect, for example experience alone can make content helpful.
- Do provide an overall great page experience across many aspects rather than focusing on only one or two.

## Strengthen main content

- Do make sure the main content (text and media, interactive tools, user-generated contributions, tabbed or expanded sections, titles and headings) directly fulfills the page's purpose.
- Do invest real human effort and original analysis. Automatically generating pages from feeds or using generative AI for large amounts of text without manual oversight or curation represents little effort, and attributing sources doesn't replace original effort.
- Do offer unique information or perspectives that aren't already available on other websites.
- Do show the skill needed for a satisfying experience, such as clear writing, well-produced video or functional tools, while recognizing not all content requires specialized expertise.
- Do keep informational content factually accurate.

## Be transparent about Who, How and Why

- Do make it clear who created the content: add accurate bylines where readers expect them, linking to author pages with background on the author and their subject areas.
- Avoid deceptive authorship such as fabricated creator profiles, AI-generated headshots, made-up names or false credentials, because deception makes a page untrustworthy.
- Do explain how content was produced where readers would wonder, for example for product reviews: how many products were tested, the results, and how tests were conducted, with evidence such as photographs.
- Do disclose automation or AI use when it would reasonably be expected, including background on how it was used and why it was useful.
- Do create content primarily to help people; the "why" is the most important question.
- Avoid using automation, including AI generation, for the primary purpose of manipulating search rankings, because that violates Google's spam policies.
